# Sections 16–17: Rival Systems & Reputation

## Section 16: Rivals

### Rival 1: Wade Pruett — "The Competition"
**The Rival Laundromat Owner**

**Personality:** Pompous, insecure, genuinely not as smart as he thinks. Wade is a man
who studied "business" for two semesters at a community college in 1989 and has been
citing it ever since. He dresses better than the situation calls for. He drives a company
car for a company of one. He is deeply, personally threatened by Retta's success and
cannot figure out why his superior equipment and digital payment kiosk isn't winning.

**Visual design:** 32×32 sprite, slicked hair, dress shirt tucked into khakis, one
hand always on his phone. Slight sneer. When he loses, he adjusts his tie.

**Role in story:**
- Introduced in Chapter 3 (casing the building)
- Opens his rival laundromat in Chapter 7
- Files a formal complaint in Chapter 7–8
- Becomes a comedic recurring frustration throughout
- At the Jubilee finale, his sign falls on his car, which is the one unambiguously
  good thing that happens that day

**Gameplay effect:**
- **Competition mechanic:** His laundromat siphons 10–15% of new walk-in customers
  when active (Chapters 7+). Counter by keeping Laundromat Reputation high.
- **Gossip relay:** Any information Wade gets goes to Connie within 24 in-game hours.
- **Complaint filings:** Occasional formal complaint events that require a response.
- **Wade's Failure Events:** Wade's laundromat occasionally has its own crises (broken
  machines, bad review week). These can be used to deflect attention from Retta's affairs.

**Recurring events:**
- "Wade's Visit" (weekly — he watches, asks questions, is outmaneuvered)
- "Wade's Complaint" (Chapter 8+)
- "Wade's Bad Week" (random, once per game — his sign falls; his coin machine ate
  someone's $50; his kiosk glitches on opening day)

**Funny dialogue:**
> *"You know, Loretta, for a facility of this size, your throughput numbers are… actually,
> what ARE your throughput numbers? How are you doing that?"*

> *"I have analytics. I have data. I have a loyalty app. You have a tip jar and a guy
> named Roy. And yet."*

> *"The sign is FINE. The sign fell on my CAR because of WIND. There was wind."*

### Rival 2: Deputy Clem Biggs — "The Law (Mostly)"

**Personality:** Clem is not a villain. He's a good-hearted small-town deputy who does
his job with sincere dedication and a limited imagination. He cannot fathom that Retta
is doing what she's doing. His worldview does not contain grandmothers with gambling dens.
This is both his greatest strength and his greatest weakness as an antagonist.

**Visual design:** Khaki deputy uniform (early appearances), plain clothes (Chapters 6+
when investigating). Round face, sensible haircut, perpetually has a to-go cup of coffee.

**Role in story:**
- Background figure in Chapters 1–4
- Mildly curious from Chapter 4 (Roy's alley traffic)
- Actively investigating from Chapter 6
- "Plainclothes mode" from Chapter 6+
- Can be befriended (becomes soft-ally if Laundromat Reputation is 75+)
- Final appearance at the Jubilee: he is either a continuing obstacle or a helpful buffer
  depending on the relationship track

**Gameplay effect:**
- **Heat multiplier:** His presence in the building +5 Heat as long as he's inside.
- **Inspection trigger:** Above Heat 50, Clem's visits become longer and more probing.
- **Befriending system:** If Laundromat Reputation ≥ 75 AND Retta has given Clem free
  laundry at least 3 times AND the player passes the "Bingo Night" event: Clem unlocks
  as a "soft ally." Ally Clem reduces Heat gain by 2/hr and warns Retta of incoming
  official visits.
- **Plainclothes mode:** In plainclothes, Clem doesn't give the automatic camera warning.
  Kev must be Level 3+ to identify him in plainclothes.

**Recurring events:**
- "Clem's Laundry Day" (weekly after Ch.2)
- "Clem's Questions" (triggers at Heat 25, 50, 75)
- "Charity Bingo Night" befriending event (Chapter 6)
- "Plainclothes Tuesday" (Chapter 6+, random)

**Funny dialogue:**
> *"Retta, I'm not saying anything's wrong. I'm just saying things are… active here.
> More active than a laundromat usually is."*

> *"I want you to know I am not investigating you specifically. I am investigating a
> general… vibe. That I have. About this area."*

> *"(Winning at bingo, plainclothes) THIS IS THE BEST NIGHT OF MY WEEK."*

### Rival 3: Connie Dupree — "The Voice of Peavine"

**Personality:** Connie is not malicious. She is simply the town's information network,
and she regards the management of that network as a civic duty. She collects details the
way other people collect figurines — carefully, displayed prominently, and not for any
specific purpose but because it feels wrong not to. She is the reason Heat events escalate
from "minor suspicion" to "full town council awareness."

**Visual design:** Church-lady chic. Hair done every Tuesday. Always has a phone in one
hand and a prayer bulletin in the other. Moves as if she is perpetually on her way
somewhere important.

**Role in story:**
- Introduced in Chapter 2 (does laundry, observes everything)
- Gossip Meter becomes a gameplay element in Chapter 5
- Files the first town council concern in Chapter 9
- At the Jubilee: can be neutralized by giving her an official "Special Guest" role,
  at which point she becomes Retta's most enthusiastic publicist

**Gameplay effect:**
- **Gossip Meter:** Independent sub-meter (0–100). Fills based on her observations.
  At 30/60/100: Heat event triggers. Meter resets after each event.
- **Wade relay:** Connie talks to Wade. Wade's complaints are stronger when Connie's
  Gossip Meter is high.
- **Church network:** High Connie gossip can affect front customer traffic (–5% when
  the church ladies are concerned).
- **Connie as ally:** If neutralized at the Jubilee, Connie's gossip goes the other
  direction entirely — she becomes the laundromat's biggest word-of-mouth advertiser.

**Recurring events:**
- "Connie's Visit" (every 3 in-game days)
- "Connie's Question" (at Gossip Meter 50)
- "The Prayer Request" (Gossip Meter 80+)
- "Connie's Corner" (town council letter at Gossip 100)

**Funny dialogue:**
> *"I'm not here to cause trouble. I just like to know things. Is that traffic through
> the back? I only mention it because I care."*

> *"I put you in the bulletin. The positive section. For now."*

> *"(At the Jubilee, ally mode) Loretta Hollis runs the best community entertainment
> venue in Peavine County and I have been saying so for months."* (She has not said this previously.)

### Rival 4: Gerald Finch — "The Inspector"

**Personality:** Gerald does not think of himself as an obstacle. He is a helpful public
servant who ensures that Peavine's commercial establishments meet basic standards of health,
safety, and cleanliness. He is also a man who genuinely enjoys his clipboard. He has
three clipboards. He has favorites.

**Visual design:** Pale blue dress shirt, pressed slacks, plastic badge, clip-on ID.
Thin-rimmed glasses. Walks with his clipboard held slightly away from his body as if
it might need to capture information at any moment.

**Role in story:**
- Scheduled annual visit in Chapter 2
- Surprise visit in Chapter 6 (delivery mix-up day)
- Full double-inspection in Chapter 9 (council concern period)
- At the Jubilee: Gerald is checking the food booth; he is, briefly, everywhere

**Gameplay effect:**
- **Inspection events:** Timed challenges (90 seconds to clean/cover/redirect).
- **Violations:** Each unresolved violation from an inspection adds to Heat. Three
  consecutive clean inspections with no violations make Gerald a "trusted" contact
  — inspections become shorter and he alerts Retta 24 in-game hours in advance.
- **Gerald's Curiosity:** Above Heat 75, Gerald starts inspecting the back area.
  Otherwise, he's front-room only.

**Recurring events:**
- "Gerald's Annual Inspection" (once per game cycle, predictable)
- "Gerald's Surprise Visit" (random from Chapter 6+)
- "Gerald's New Clipboard" (cosmetic joke event: he has a new clipboard; he's excited)

**Funny dialogue:**
> *"Ms. Hollis, I noticed the lint trap in Dryer 3 was at 87% capacity on my last visit.
> It is now at 92%. I find that concerning. Not 'write-up' concerning. But I am noting it."*

> *"What is this room used for?" / "Storage." / "Of what?" / "Things." / "…I see."*

> *"(After excellent inspection) This establishment is a credit to Gipson Street.
> I don't say that often. I said it once before. It was 2009. To a tire shop."*

### Rival 5: Roy Macabee (Unlucky Gambler Side)

**Note:** Roy is a regular, not a true rival. But in certain events he plays an
"unlucky gambler" role that creates gameplay pressure.

**Roy in this mode:** Roy goes on a cold streak. He loses four sessions in a row.
He is convinced the universe is against him (his lucky socks incident doesn't help).
He starts staying later, betting higher trying to recover losses. This is Roy's
only selfish habit. He is not a problem gambler narratively — it's played for warmth
and comedy, not darkness. He just gets a little stubborn.

**Gameplay effect:** Roy in losing streak → bets higher → stays later → backroom runs
longer → Heat accumulates faster. Player must either gently close the session or let
Roy have his night.

**Funny dialogue (losing streak):**
> *"I'm fine. I'm absolutely fine. One more hand. I'm not leaving on a seven."*

### Rival 6: Pastor Wendell Tharp — "The Well-Meaning Problem"

**Personality:** Wendell is a good person. He is genuinely concerned about his community's
wellbeing. He is not trying to cause trouble. He simply has a very broad definition of
"community wellbeing" that includes knowing what's happening at the laundromat on Wednesday
nights, and a theological framework that regards gambling as a moral concern.

He is also charmed by Retta. This creates a constant tension where he almost disapproves
of her and then she says something that makes him laugh and he forgets what he was
almost disapproving.

**Role in story:** Recurring medium-intensity visitor from Chapter 4 onward. He and
Retta have a warm but navigated relationship. She respects him. He respects her.
Neither lets that stop them from their respective agendas.

**Gameplay effect:**
- Visits once per 4 in-game days in mid-to-late game.
- His presence alone is a soft +3 Heat event (nosy, but benign).
- Can be converted: run the charity bingo (Option A of his event). He then
  acts as a buffer against Connie's gossip ("Pastor vouches for the place").

**Funny dialogue:**
> *"Loretta, I want to be very clear that I'm not here to investigate anything.
> I'm here as a neighbor. And a minister. Those two things can coexist."*

> *"That's a bingo board. I recognize bingo boards. We use them at the church."*

> *"(After charity bingo event) You know, Loretta, I thought about this a great deal
> and I've decided I'm going to pray for clarity on the matter. In the meantime,
> the cobbler was very good."*

### Rival 7: Nadine Pruitt — "Wade's Nosiest Asset"

**Personality:** Wade's older sister, who lives in the neighborhood. She doesn't even
need to try — she's just everywhere. She's at every community event. She's on every
block. She knows everyone. She feeds everything she sees to Wade. She means no harm.
She just loves Peavine more than Peavine can sometimes handle.

**Role in story:** Background character who becomes a recurring minor event trigger from
Chapter 6+. She's not as focused as Connie but she's less predictable — she shows up
in the alley because she's looking for her cat, not because she's suspicious.

**Gameplay effect:**
- Random alley appearance event (Chapter 6+): small Heat spike (+5).
- "Cat Event" — her cat (Biscuit) enters the alley and she follows. Player must redirect.
- Can be neutralized with a monthly "Neighbor Discount" ($5 off laundry). Costs $5/month
  but keeps her from the alley and she tells Wade the laundromat is "very friendly."

**Funny dialogue:**
> *"Has anyone seen a tabby cat? About this big? Very judgmental expression?
> She likes warm buildings. (looks at alley door) Oh. Does she come through here?"*

## Section 17: Reputation System

Three parallel reputation bars shape how the game plays. They are all visible in the
top bar (expandable detail view). None of them can go negative — they floor at 0.

---

### Bar 1: Laundromat Reputation ⭐
**Range:** 0–100
**Display name:** "Peavine Rating"
**Icon:** Gold star

**What it represents:** How the public front laundromat is perceived by the town.
Clean machines, happy customers, no drama.

**Gains:**
- Every customer who leaves satisfied: +0.2
- Health inspection passed cleanly: +5
- Positive newspaper article: +10–25
- Charity event: +10–20
- Retta calms an angry customer: +1
- Decor upgrades (each level): +3–8
- Completing chapter goals: +5–15

**Losses:**
- Customer leaves angry: –1
- Health inspection violation: –3 each
- Broken machine visible for >5 min: –1
- Flood/fire event: –5
- Wade's successful complaint: –10
- Heat-100 event: –8

**What it changes:**
- **0–30:** Very few walk-in customers. Barely surviving.
- **31–60:** Average traffic. Some regulars. Normal business.
- **61–80:** Above-average traffic. Longer stays. Better tips. Gerald eases up.
- **81–100:** Retta's Laundromat is *the* place to do laundry in Peavine. Customers
  come from neighboring towns. Wade's business suffers noticeably. Connie says positive
  things. Clem leaves his uniform at home and just does his laundry.

**Special at 100:** "Town's Favorite" badge unlocked. Small permanent badge on the
exterior sign tile. Clem gifts Retta a "Best Small Business" certificate. He printed it himself.

---

### Bar 2: Backroom Reputation 🃏
**Range:** 0–100
**Display name:** "Word on the Street"
**Icon:** Playing card / small neon sign

**What it represents:** How The Lint Trap is perceived by the backroom community.
Good nights, fair games, good snacks, great host.

**Gains:**
- Successful bingo session: +2
- Poker session runs smoothly: +3
- High Roller visits and leaves happy: +10
- A new regular converted: +5
- Carlotta's upsell bonus nights: +2
- Snack bar well-stocked: +1 per session
- Jackpot paid out fairly: +4

**Losses:**
- Sore Loser scene not managed: –5
- Session cut short by heat event: –3
- Snack bar empty during session: –4
- A regular gets embarrassed in front of new visitors: –6
- Fake Rich Guy's unpaid tab not collected: –3

**What it changes:**
- **0–30:** Only Roy and Darlene show up. Very small operation.
- **31–50:** Word is spreading. New visitors weekly. Better buy-ins.
- **51–75:** The Lint Trap has a reputation. People travel for it. High Roller visits.
  Carlotta can run premium nights.
- **76–100:** The Lint Trap is a legend. VIP Corner is always full. Weekly income from
  backroom rivals or exceeds front room. Minnie Vashti is now effectively the backroom's
  benevolent patron. Roy is finally winning.

**Special at 100:** "The Real Reason People Come to Peavine" secret achievement unlocked.

### Bar 3: Town Suspicion 🌡️
**Range:** 0–100
**Display name:** "The Buzz"
**Icon:** Speech bubble with a question mark

**Note:** This is equivalent to the Heat Meter displayed in a reputation context. They
share the same underlying value. Town Suspicion tracks the *social awareness* side of Heat,
while the Heat icon tracks the *immediate risk* side. For gameplay purposes, they are one meter
displayed in two ways: as an orange thermometer (active risk) and as a blue speech bubble
(social standing).

**What it represents:** How suspicious the town is about Retta specifically.
Separate from Heat level — it's about community perception, not immediate cop-presence.

**Gains:** Same as Heat gains (see Heat System doc).
**Losses:** Same as Heat reduction mechanics.

**What it changes:**
- **0–25:** Nobody suspects anything. Business is normal. Retta is a pillar of the community.
- **26–50:** People have opinions. Some customers are curious. Connie is activated.
  Wade feels emboldened.
- **51–75:** The town is divided. Half think Retta runs a fun community club. Half think
  something is up. Both sides gossip.
- **76–100:** Everyone suspects. Nobody can prove anything. It becomes its own kind of
  social cover — at this point even the cops are confused by the contradiction between
  the perfectly clean laundromat and the thriving backroom.

**Interaction with other bars:**
- High Laundromat Reputation (80+) suppresses Suspicion gain rate by 30%.
- High Backroom Reputation (75+) starts generating Heat/Suspicion on its own — word
  spreads too well.
- At Laundromat Rep 100 + Suspicion below 25: Retta is effectively untouchable by normal
  complaints. Wade's complaints are dismissed. Gerald's inspections are brief.
  Clem is just doing laundry.
